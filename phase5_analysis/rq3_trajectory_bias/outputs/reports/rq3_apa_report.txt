1. DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The comparison set is every included element on screens a trajectory actually
visited, not the whole corpus. Holding the screen fixed isolates element
selection from screen selection, which is what the hypothesis concerns. Touched
elements are inferred by point-in-bounds hit testing, because AndroidControl
actions record coordinates and no element reference.

reading is that benchmark tasks are easier than the app population they are
drawn from, and that the gap is an accessibility artifact rather than a property
of the task design. Reported agent accuracy would then be an optimistic estimate
of performance on arbitrary screens.

NOTES, ASSUMPTIONS AND LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Notes
  1. Permutation p-values use the (r+1)/(m+1) convention and can never be
     exactly zero, which is the honest way to report a test whose null was never
     matched.

Limitations
  1. Touched elements are inferred from coordinates, not read from the data. A
     systematic bias in the hit-testing rule, for instance a preference for
     larger targets, would propagate into this result. The reported hit rate and
     the clickable-only comparison are the two guards against that.
  2. Elements within a screen are not independent, and one screen can contribute
     many background elements and one touched element. The permutation test
     respects the observed marginals but not the nesting; a screen-level cluster
     bootstrap would be the stronger design and is the natural extension for the
     journal version.
